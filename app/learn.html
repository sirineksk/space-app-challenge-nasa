The Sun always stays low (1.5° tilt)

The Sun never climbs high above flat ground, so hills cast shadows kilometres long. A 100 m hill casts a shadow almost 4 km long.

L = h / tan(e) ≈ 38·h at e = 1.5°

Earth rises and sets slowly (libration)

The Moon wobbles slightly, so Earth slowly rises and sets near the horizon, and mountains can hide it for up to two weeks.

Libration in latitude ±6.7° over 27.2 days

The Moon's curve hides far mountains

The Moon is small, so the ground curves away fast. Distant mountains sit lower than they look: 50 km away, about 720 m lower.

No air: no twilight, black shadows

Without air there is no dusk glow and shadows are pitch black. The Sun moves so slowly it can take hours to slide behind a hill.
